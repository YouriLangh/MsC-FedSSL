%The authors of "FedMatch.pdf" found that their proposed method, FedMatch, significantly outperforms both local SSL and the naive combination of FL with SSL algorithms. They observed that naive combination models (e.g., FedAvg-FixMatch, FedProx-FixMatch, FedAvg-UDA, FedProx-UDA) suffer significantly from the "forgetting issue" and their performance continues to deteriorate after a certain number of communication rounds, particularly in the more challenging "labels-at-server" scenario. This issue is primarily caused by base models failing to perform disjoint learning properly, leading to inter-task interference between knowledge learned from labeled and unlabeled data. They also noted that baseline models like FedProx-UDA/FixMatch show performance degradation even when the amount of labeled data increases, indicating that naive combinations can fail to learn properly from both labeled and unlabeled data within the FL framework.
% Similarly, the paper "Federated semi-supervised learning with contrastive representations against.pdf" also highlights the limitations of these naive combinations. It notes that the test accuracies of "naive combinations methods FedAvg-FixMatch, FedProx-FixMatch, FedAvg-UDA and FedProx-UDA are almost equivalent to or inferior to the performance of Server-SL, especially in the presence of noisy labels". The subpar performance of these naive combinations is attributed to several factors:
They simply combine FL with SSL algorithms without considering the impact of noisy labels on the model's performance.
• When client-side data distributions are heterogeneous or labels are noisy, pseudo-labels generated by SSL algorithms are often inaccurate or incorrect, introducing bias during local training that propagates and accumulates in later aggregation rounds, leading to global model performance deterioration.
• FL algorithms like FedAvg and FedProx assume reliable model updates from each client, but this assumption fails with noisy labels, causing local models to generate biased updates that skew the global model and affect its generalisation ability.
• SSL algorithms such as FixMatch and UDA are inherently sensitive to label noise, as they rely on confidence thresholds to select pseudo-labels, assuming high-confidence pseudo-labels are correct, even when they are not.
Other sources also support the idea that direct application of SSL in FL is problematic:
• "(SemiFed) Semi-supervised Federated Learning with Consistency and.pdf" states that it is "not trivial to marry centralized SSL techniques with FL training due to its distinctive requirements, rendering a direct application of SSL impractical for real FL settings". This source also explains that challenges like non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) data among clients, where labeled and unlabeled data may come from different distributions or contain mismatched classes, can severely impact SSL algorithm design.
• "Dynamic Class-Balanced Threshold Federated Semi-Supervised Learning.pdf" mentions that most previous FSSL methods using traditional fixed thresholds (like FixMatch) often result in much data being discarded in early training stages and do not fundamentally resolve the challenges of FSSL, particularly with limited labeled data and non-IID problems.
• "FedECG.pdf" notes that SSL, while providing pseudo-labels, can also generate false ones, reducing model accuracy, and that non-IID data further impacts model performance in an FL setting.
• "Towards Utilizing Unlabeled Data in Federated Learning. A Survey and Prospective.pdf" highlights that if labeled and unlabeled data belong to significantly different domains, SSL algorithms degrade, necessitating their combination with techniques to address domain discrepancy in FL.
• "SelfFed.pdf" mentions that existing semi-supervised and self-supervised based federated learning strategies "suffer from performance degradation due to label scarcity and diverse data distributions, i.e., data heterogeneity".
