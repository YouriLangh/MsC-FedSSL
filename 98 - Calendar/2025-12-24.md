DDRFed requires pretraining of labeled model and stuff. 


**DDRFed (Wang et al., 2025):** This method assumes that the best way to handle Non-IID data is to generate synthetic data that follows a global distribution. Consequently, its setup requires a **"preheating phase"** where each client trains an unconditional diffusion model for 40 rounds. The server then generates a synthetic dataset and distributes it evenly to clients to mitigate data skew before the actual FSSL training starts.

• **FedCR (Mao et al., 2025):** This method assumes that labels at the server are inherently noisy. Its setup involves **700 rounds of server-side pre-training** using these noisy labels to establish a foundational model and contrastive representation module. Only after this server-side foundation is built does the model begin its 200 rounds of federated communication with unlabeled clients.

• **CBAFed (Li et al., 2023):** Assuming that catastrophic forgetting is a primary threat, this method includes a **"warm up stage"** where models are trained exclusively on labeled clients using a specific "residual weight connection" to ensure the initial global model is robust before involving unlabeled clients.







## 1. What a sensitivity analysis is _supposed_ to demonstrate

A sensitivity analysis is **not** about novelty of numbers.  
It is about **response behavior** of a system when _controlled inputs are varied_.

A committee expects to see answers to these questions:

1. **Which variables are varied?**
    
2. **Are they varied systematically and independently?**
    
3. **Does performance change monotonically or non-monotonically?**
    
4. **Are there interaction effects?**
    
5. **Does variance / stability change, not just the mean?**
    
6. **Is there a dominant sensitivity axis?**
    

Now let’s check your section _point by point_.

## 3. Do the results _respond_ meaningfully to those variations?

This is the most important criterion.  
And here the answer is a **very strong yes**.

### Sensitivity to heterogeneity

You show:

- consistent degradation as α decreases
    
- quantified drops (−21.9%, −26%)
    
- increased variance
    
- instability in learning curves
    
- divergence-like behavior
    

This is **textbook heterogeneity sensitivity**.

### Sensitivity to label budget

You show:

- monotonic improvement in accuracy
    
- diminishing returns for FixMatch
    
- disproportionate gains for supervised
    
- regime change where supervised overtakes FixMatch
    

That is **exactly what sensitivity to supervision should look like**.

---

### Interaction effects (this is where your work shines)

You do **not** treat axes independently:

You show that:

- increasing labels helps under IID / α=0.5
    
- increasing labels under α=0.1 increases _variance_
    
- heterogeneity changes _how_ labels help
    

That is a **second-order sensitivity effect**, and many papers don’t show this at all.

---

## 4. Do you analyze _stability_, not just accuracy?

Yes — and this is critical.

You analyze:

- variance across rounds
    
- learning dynamics
    
- convergence speed
    
- saturation vs prolonged learning
    
- confidence vs correctness mismatch (AUROC vs F1)
    

This moves your work from:

> “performance reporting”  
> to  
> **system behavior analysis**

That is _exactly_ what a sensitivity study is meant to do.
